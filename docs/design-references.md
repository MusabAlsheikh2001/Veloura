# Design references: what to learn, and what not to copy

Studied from public page text and first screens (not a full visual or motion audit).
Purpose: learn standards, not replicate layouts, copy, or assets.

## Instrument (instrument.com)
- Opens with one plain claim: "digital-first design agency where creativity meets technology".
- Splits services into brand, product and marketing, then says they should tell one story.
- Client and award rows are real, verifiable proof. Veloura must not add awards or logos it does not hold.

## MetaLab (metalab.com)
- A single blunt statement, "We make interfaces", set very large.
- Client marquee and a numbered work index keep the page moving without extra copy.

## Clay (clay.global)
- Four service pillars (Branding, Digital Products, Websites, Development), each explained in two or three plain sentences.
- Brand and interface are presented as one practice, not two departments.

## Work & Co (work.co)
- Practice areas, outcomes, process and select clients are separate, scannable sections.
- Case work is introduced with a short "Learn more" link, not long marketing text.

## Basement (basement.studio)
- Bold one-line positioning, then featured projects with the business problem named.
- Projects are presented as stories with a clear challenge. Veloura should do the same only with verified outcomes.

## Basic / Dept (basicagency.com)
- Opens with a moving reel of work rather than a static claim.
- Credibility comes from named industry recognition, which Veloura does not yet have.

## What this means for Veloura
1. Keep one strong sentence in the hero, as in MetaLab and Instrument.
2. Give each service a short, plain explanation of the problem it solves (Clay).
3. Present each portfolio project as a short story: the brief, what was built, and what it does. Show only facts the client has provided.
4. Use real proof only: real logos with permission, real awards, real results. Never invent them.
5. Motion at Basement's level is a separate build. It needs its own plan and time.
